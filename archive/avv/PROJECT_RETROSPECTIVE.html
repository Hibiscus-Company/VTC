The mission

What the number means

The task is per-scene novel-view synthesis: from a handful of drone or phone photos of a scene (plus its COLMAP reconstruction), render the exact camera poses of a withheld test set. A single deterministic scorer grades every submission on three axes:

score = 100 · [ 0.4·(1−LPIPSvgg) + 0.3·SSIM + 0.3·PSNR/50 ]

The weighting is the whole strategy: perceptual quality (LPIPS) is 40%, so sharpness and structure matter more than raw pixel error. We competed across two datasets. Set 1 — 13 drone-captured telecom towers, dense urban rooftops — where we climbed from 74.3 to 78.6 over nine rounds. Then the organizers shipped Set 2 on 16 July: five more towers plus two genuinely different indoor video scenes — a bonsai on a glossy black glass table, and an office chair shot handheld on a phone. Set 2 reset the board; top-1 fell from 86.1 to 80.6, and the whole campaign restarted around those two hard video scenes.

Architecture

The pipeline that ships a submission

Every scene is routed by its own COLMAP metadata — no hard-coded scene names, so the pipeline generalizes to scenes we’ve never seen. The two branches below are the load-bearing discovery of the whole campaign: drone towers and indoor videos want opposite renderers.

The quiet hero is the per-scene eval-split selector. Instead of trusting a recipe tuned on other scenes, it punches isolated holes into each scene’s own training set — mimicking the exact structure of the withheld test — trains candidate recipes, and picks the winner on the held-out holes using the real competition metric. It’s a name-free router: every scene, seen or unseen, selects its own recipe from its own data. That single mechanism found the fix that unlocked Set 2.

Evolution

How the architecture actually changed

Eight generations, each one a real shift in how models were trained or combined. The delta is the leaderboard gain attributable to that generation.

G1Base 3DGS + importance-gated densificationbaselineClassic Gaussian splatting with a densification gate (metric_gate), an aggressive gradient threshold (“g15”), and a VGG-LPIPS loss term — the perceptual axis, wired in early because it’s 40% of the score.
G2Ensemble of gate configs (pixel-mean)+1.82Averaging several models’ renders cancels reconstruction noise while keeping the GT frequency response — unlike blur, it raises PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS at once. The single biggest early lever.
G33DGUT — train on virgin distorted pixels+0.90Distorted-space rasterization (unscented transform) trains on the original photos instead of once-resampled undistorted ones. As a decorrelated ensemble member with family-weighting, it beat a whole 3-model classic ensemble.
G4Lens-field correction+0.73COLMAP’s single-k1 lens under-fits reality; renders sit a fraction of a pixel off the photos. We fit that fixed sub-pixel field as mean dense optical flow on train photos only, and warp the renders. Legal, and it buys PSNR and SSIM together.
G5Capacity + schedule scale-up (60k / 8M)+0.18Longer training and more gaussians per member. Diminishing — 5M→8M was only +0.085 dB — but reliably positive and stackable.
G6Per-scene eval-split model selectionmethodThe Set-2 pivot. A within-scene train/eval split that mimics the hidden test lets us select recipes per scene on a legal proxy — and calibrate how much an eval win will actually transfer.
G7Bonsai collapse fix (churn-stop)+3.62The glossy-glass scene collapsed to fog — 5M near-transparent gaussians. Root cause: MCMC noise/relocation churn running too long on view-inconsistent reflections. Stopping the churn early rescued it. The largest single gain of the campaign.
G8Antialiased mode for pinhole video+0.139The video scenes have zero lens distortion (k1=0), so the UT path bought nothing and silently forfeited gsplat’s Mip/antialiasing filter. Dropping --ut unlocked it — +0.49 per video scene, and it transferred at 0.71× (structural changes transfer; recipe tweaks don’t).

Postmortem

Mistakes & errors

The honest half of the log. Two classes: strategy calls that graded wrong, and operational failures that burned GPU-hours.

R13 gates — wrong sign

Shipped Set-1-tuned family weights onto Set-2 members that were two generations stronger. Predicted +0.4, graded −0.60. Violated a rule I had written myself: re-sweep weights whenever member quality changes.−0.60 · 1 slot · ~1 GPU-day

Eval→test overestimation

Repeatedly read eval gains at face value. Recipe nudges actually compress ~0.3× to the leaderboard (R12: predicted +0.13, graded +0.04). Only structural changes transfer near 1×. Now a hard planning rule.3 rounds of inflated forecasts

The set -u + conda hooks

Three separate script deaths on conda activate/deactivate hooks referencing unbound env vars under strict mode. Each stranded idle GPUs before I caught it.~6 GPU-hours idle · fixed

Monitors on a Windows mount

Log watchers used tail -F on a drive with no inotify; they silently died and hid progress. Switched every monitor to polling.false “stalled” signals

Oversize zip

Antialiased renders compress worse; the quality ladder overshot the 350 MB cap by 15 KB and failed verification. Fixed with an explicit size margin.1 rebuild

Runaway diagnostic runs

An experiment ran VGG-LPIPS every step at 8M gaussians — 8½ hours for 8k steps before I killed it. A capacity chase (16M) burned 13h to an OOM.~20 GPU-hours

The through-line: my predictions were reliable on structural changes (the collapse fix, the field, antialiasing) and unreliable on magnitude for small tweaks and novel compositions. The fix wasn’t better guessing — it was making the deterministic scorer the arbiter via single-scene A/B swaps, so every ship decision is measured, not forecast.

Ceilings

Limits we still face

The 3.77-point gap

Top-1 jumped there in one step — the signature of a bug fix, not a method. We have no visible lever that closes it, and two independent audits agree none exists in the reachable set.

Transfer compression

Recipe tuning discounts ~0.3× eval→LB. The remaining tunable surface is therefore worth fractions of a point per round — grind, not breakthrough.

Video content ceiling

Glossy-glass reflections are view-inconsistent; the chair is genuinely motion-/DoF-blurred; exposure drifts 25%. The two video scenes sit ~10 points below the towers and that’s where the deficit lives.

Capacity is closed

5M→8M gaussians bought +0.085 dB; 16M is impractical. More splats is not the answer on this data.

Registration cave sealed

A per-image warp oracle is worth +2.89 — but it’s non-transferable and skirts the rules. Only the ~+0.7 of it captured by the legal train-fit field survives.

Operational surface

Conda hooks, network-mount monitors, and session-wake latency each cost real GPU time. Hardened, but the environment stays fragile.

What’s left

Challenges that remain

The reliable levers are small and known: a third tower seed (~+0.1 via ensemble decorrelation) and a sharper per-scene combine (~+0.05). Both are running or queued. Beyond them sit two coin-flip structural bets — rolling-shutter modeling for the handheld chair (but it forfeits the antialiasing win, so net-uncertain), and a from-scratch different splatting variant (Mip-Splatting or 2DGS surfels for the glass table). Neither has a strong prior; both are 1–2 GPU-day gambles.

So the endgame is disciplined, not heroic. Ship only what a single-scene swap has proven on the real scorer. Freeze the recipe two days before the 30 July deadline. Assemble the final submission as the per-scene argmax over everything graded — the best version of each scene, regardless of which round produced it. The honest target is 77.0–77.3 and a defended second place, not the summit. The campaign’s real product isn’t the score — it’s a fully-mapped search space: we know where the points are, where they aren’t, and exactly how much each remaining move is worth.
